Privacy Policy

Last updated: 19 November 2026

In short: Hourmint does not collect, store or share any of your data. Everything you enter stays on your Mac.

What Hourmint stores

Hourmint stores what you enter: your business details, clients, projects, rates, time entries, invoices, settings, and an optional logo image. All of this is saved in a database on your Mac, inside the app's own folder. Hourmint also keeps up to 7 daily backups of that database and a small log file for troubleshooting, both on your Mac.

What Hourmint sends

Nothing. Hourmint has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and it does not send your data to us or to anyone else.

The only time Hourmint goes online is to check GitHub for a newer version, when it starts and every 12 hours. That request includes only what any web request includes (your IP address and the app version) and none of your data. It is handled under GitHub's privacy statement.

Files you export

PDF invoices, timesheets, CSV and JSON exports and backup files are saved only where you choose. What you do with them, such as emailing an invoice to a client, is up to you.

Your clients' information

Client names, contacts and addresses you enter are stored on your Mac in the same way and are never sent to us.

Deleting your data

You can delete clients, projects, entries and invoices in the app. To remove everything, delete the app and its data folder:

~/Library/Application Support/app.hourmint.desktop

This website

This website uses no cookies, no analytics and no third-party scripts or fonts. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, which may keep standard server logs under GitHub's privacy statement.

Children

Hourmint is a business tool and does not knowingly collect data from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date. If Hourmint ever starts collecting any data, it will ask you first.

Contact

Questions about privacy or support: open an issue at github.com/abdurrahmanriyad/hourmint/issues. Hourmint is open source, so you can also read exactly what it does with your data in the source code.
